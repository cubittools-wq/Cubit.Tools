How to remove VAT from a price

VAT is added to the net price, so it has to be taken off by dividing, not by subtracting a percentage of the total.

Net price = Price including VAT / (1 + Rate / 100)

At the standard rate that means dividing by 1.2. At the reduced rate, divide by 1.05.

Worked example

A receipt shows £120 including VAT at 20%. The price before VAT is £100 and the VAT is £20.

The mistake to avoid

Taking 20% off a VAT-inclusive price gives the wrong answer. 20% of £120 is £24, but the VAT in £120 is only £20, because the 20% was charged on the net price of £100. A quick check: at the standard rate, the VAT is always one sixth of the total.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the total price, including VAT.
  2. Choose the VAT rate that was charged.
  3. The calculator shows the price before VAT and the amount of VAT in the total.

The VAT fraction

Accountants often use a shortcut called the VAT fraction to find the VAT in a total. At 20% the fraction is 1/6, so the VAT is the total divided by 6. At 5% the fraction is 1/21. The calculator gives the same answer by dividing the total by 1 plus the rate.

Why is the VAT not 20% of the total?

Because 20% was added to the net price, which is smaller than the total. As a share of the total, VAT at the standard rate is 16.67%.

When you need to reverse VAT

  • Working out your margin from a retail price that includes VAT.
  • Recording the net cost of a purchase when the receipt only shows the total.
  • Comparing a supplier's ex-VAT quote with a price that includes VAT.
