What is the Bradford Factor?

The Bradford Factor is a score some employers use to measure sickness absence. It gives extra weight to frequent short absences, on the view that these disrupt work more than one longer absence of the same total length.

Bradford Factor formula

Bradford Factor = S x S x D

S is the number of separate spells of absence and D is the total number of days absent, usually over a rolling 52 weeks.

Worked example

An employee is off on 4 separate occasions totalling 9 days. Their score is 144 points. One single absence of the same nine days would score 9, and nine separate days off would score 729.

Trigger points

Employers set their own thresholds, so there is no standard scale. A policy might call for an informal chat at one score and a formal review at a higher one. Check your own organisation's absence policy for the figures it uses.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of separate spells of absence in the last 52 weeks.
  2. Enter the total number of working days lost in those spells.
  3. Read off the score.

How the same days give different scores

Use it with care

The score is a prompt for a conversation, not a decision in itself. It takes no account of the reason for an absence. Applying it rigidly to absences linked to disability or pregnancy can lead to unlawful discrimination, so employers normally make adjustments for those cases.
