What is the payback period?

The payback period is the time it takes for the cash an investment brings in to equal what it cost. Businesses use it as a quick test of risk: the sooner the money is back, the less can go wrong in the meantime.

Payback period formula

Payback period = Initial investment / Net cash inflow per year

This assumes the same inflow every year.

Worked example

A new machine costs £24,000 and saves £7,500 a year. It pays for itself in 3.2 years, or 38.4 months.

Strengths and limits

  • Simple and quick. It needs only two figures.
  • Ignores what happens next. An investment that pays back in three years and then keeps earning for ten looks the same as one that stops after three.
  • Ignores the time value of money. Cash received later is worth less than cash today. Net present value takes that into account.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good payback period?

Shorter is safer. Many small businesses look for two to three years on equipment, but the right limit depends on how long the asset will last.
