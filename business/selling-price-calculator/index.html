Pricing from a target margin

If your business needs a set margin to cover its overheads, the price has to be worked back from the cost. Adding the margin percentage on top of the cost is a common mistake: adding 40% to a cost gives a margin of only 28.57%.

Selling price formula

Selling price = Cost / (1 - Margin / 100)

Worked example

An item costs you £30 and you want a margin of 40%. The selling price needs to be £50. Each sale then earns £20, which is a markup of 66.67% on the cost.

Before you set the price

  • Count every cost. Include delivery to you, packaging, payment fees and marketplace commission, not only the purchase price.
  • Add VAT afterwards. If you are VAT registered, the result is the price before VAT.
  • Check the market. The calculator shows what you need to charge. Customers and competitors decide whether you can.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the full cost of one item.
  2. Enter the margin you want as a percentage of the selling price.
  3. The calculator shows the price to charge, the profit on each sale and the markup that price represents.

If you sell through a marketplace that charges commission on the selling price, treat the commission as part of your target. For a 30% margin after a 15% fee, enter 45%.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I enter a margin of 100%?

A 100% margin would mean the product cost nothing. As the target margin gets close to 100%, the price needed rises without limit.

Is this the same as a markup calculator?

No. A markup calculator adds a percentage of the cost. This one finds the price where a chosen percentage of the price is profit.
