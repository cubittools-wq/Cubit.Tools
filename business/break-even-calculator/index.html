What is the break-even point?

The break-even point is the number of sales at which your income exactly covers your costs. Below it you make a loss. Every sale above it adds to profit.

Break-even formula

Break-even units = Fixed costs / (Selling price - Variable cost per unit)

The amount in brackets is the contribution: what each sale puts towards your fixed costs.

Worked example

Your fixed costs are £5,000 a month. You sell at £25 and each unit costs you £15, so each sale contributes £10. You need to sell 500 units a month to break even, which is £12,500 of sales.

Fixed and variable costs

  • Fixed costs stay the same however much you sell: rent, salaries, insurance, software subscriptions.
  • Variable costs rise with each sale: stock, packaging, postage, card fees and marketplace commission.

How to lower your break-even point

There are three ways: raise the price, cut the variable cost of each unit, or reduce fixed costs. Change the figures above to see which makes the most difference to your business.

Why is no result shown?

If the variable cost is the same as or higher than the price, each sale loses money and no number of sales will cover the fixed costs.
