What is sell-through rate?

Sell-through rate is the percentage of the stock you bought that you have sold in a given period. Retailers use it to judge how well a product, range or season is selling, usually over a month.

Sell-through rate formula

Sell-through rate = Units sold / Units received x 100

Worked example

You took delivery of 500 units at the start of the month and sold 320. Your sell-through rate is 64%, with 180 units still in stock.

Reading the result

A high rate means stock is turning into cash quickly, and may mean you could have sold more. A low rate means money is tied up in stock that might need discounting to clear. What counts as good depends on the product: fashion lines are expected to sell through within a season, while spare parts may sell slowly for years.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of units you received or had in stock at the start of the period.
  2. Enter the number sold in the same period.
  3. Read off the sell-through rate and the units left.

Count units, not pounds, and use the same period for both figures.

Acting on the result

  • Above target: reorder before you run out, and consider whether the price is too low.
  • On target: the buy was about right. Use the same quantity as a starting point next time.
  • Below target: improve the listing or display first, then promote the line, and mark it down if it still does not move. Stock that sits costs money in storage and ties up cash you could spend on lines that sell.

Tracking sell-through by product and by week shows which lines to back early in a season, when there is still time to act.

Frequently asked questions

What period should I measure?

Use the same period every time, so results can be compared. A month is the most common.

How is it different from stock turnover?

Sell-through looks at one delivery or period and counts units. Stock turnover compares the cost of everything sold with the average value of stock held, usually across a year.
