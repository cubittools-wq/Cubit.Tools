What is gross profit margin?

Gross profit margin is the share of your selling price that is left once you have paid for the goods themselves. It ignores overheads such as rent, wages and marketing, so it tells you how profitable the product is before the costs of running the business are taken off.

The calculator works for a single item or for a whole period: enter one product's cost and price, or your total cost of goods sold and total sales.

Gross profit margin formula

Gross profit = Selling price - Cost of goods

Gross profit margin = Gross profit / Selling price x 100

Worked example

You buy a lamp for £60 and sell it for £100 before VAT. Your gross profit is £40, so your gross margin is 40%. The same sale has a markup of 66.67%, because markup is measured against the cost and margin against the selling price.

What is a good gross margin?

It depends on the trade. Grocers and electronics retailers often work on low margins and high volumes, while clothing, furniture and software businesses usually need much higher margins to cover their overheads. The most useful comparison is with your own figures from earlier periods, and with the margin you need to cover your fixed costs.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include VAT?

No. If you are VAT registered, use prices and costs without VAT, because the VAT you charge is not your income.

Can gross margin be more than 100%?

No. Margin is a share of the selling price, so it can never pass 100%. Markup can, and often does.

Why is my margin negative?

The cost is higher than the selling price, so each sale loses money before overheads.
