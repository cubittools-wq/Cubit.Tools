What is revenue per employee?

Revenue per employee is a company's revenue divided by its headcount. It is a rough measure of productivity: how much income the business produces for each person it employs.

Revenue per employee formula

Revenue per employee = Annual revenue / Number of employees

Worked example

A business turns over £1,500,000 a year with 12 staff. Its revenue per employee is £125,000.

How to use the figure

The number varies widely between industries. Software and wholesale businesses can produce high revenue with few staff, while restaurants, care and retail need many more people for the same turnover. Compare yourself with similar businesses, and watch how your own figure changes from year to year.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the revenue for the last full year.
  2. Enter the average number of employees during that year, as full-time equivalents.
  3. Read off the revenue per employee.

What moves the figure

Revenue per employee rises when sales grow faster than headcount. That can come from better tools, automation, higher prices or outsourcing work that used to be done in-house. It falls when a business hires ahead of growth, which is normal for a company investing in new products or markets.

Outsourcing can flatter the figure: a firm that uses contractors and agencies will show higher revenue per employee than one doing the same work with its own staff. For a fairer comparison, look at profit per employee too.

Frequently asked question

Should contractors be included?

For a like-for-like view of productivity, yes, as full-time equivalents. Published company figures usually count employees only, so check what a benchmark includes before you compare.

Frequently asked questions

How should I count part-time staff?

Convert them to full-time equivalents. Two people each working half the week count as one.

Is higher always better?

Not on its own. A very high figure can mean the team is stretched. Look at profit per employee as well, because revenue says nothing about costs.
