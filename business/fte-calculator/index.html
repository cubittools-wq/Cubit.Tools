What is FTE?

FTE stands for full-time equivalent. It expresses a workforce of full-time and part-time people as the number of full-time staff who would work the same hours. One FTE is one person working full-time hours.

FTE formula

Part-time FTE = Total part-time hours per week / Full-time hours per week

Total FTE = Full-time employees + Part-time FTE

Worked example

You employ 14 full-time staff, and your full-time week is 37.5 hours. Your part-time staff work 180 hours a week between them, which is 4.80 FTE. Your total headcount is 18.80 FTE.

What FTE is used for

  • Budgeting and workforce planning, where hours matter more than the number of people.
  • Comparing teams or branches with different mixes of full-time and part-time staff.
  • Measures such as revenue per employee.
  • Grant applications and official returns that ask for FTE figures.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of full-time employees.
  2. Add up the weekly hours of all your part-time staff and enter the total.
  3. Enter the number of hours in your standard full-time week.

FTE for one person

To find one part-time employee's FTE, divide their weekly hours by the full-time week. Someone working 22.5 hours in an organisation with a 37.5 hour week is 0.6 FTE. The same fraction is used to work out pro rata pay and holiday: 0.6 FTE of a £30,000 salary is £18,000.

Frequently asked question

Is FTE the same as headcount?

No. Headcount is the number of people. Ten staff who each work half the week are a headcount of 10 and an FTE of 5. Budgets usually use FTE, while office space and equipment depend on headcount.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as full-time hours?

Whatever your organisation's standard full-time week is. In the UK that is commonly between 35 and 40 hours.

Can one person be more than 1 FTE?

Not normally. Overtime is usually left out, and each person counts as 1.0 at most.
