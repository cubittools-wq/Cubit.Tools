What is average order value?

Average order value, or AOV, is the average amount a customer spends each time they order. Online shops track it alongside traffic and conversion rate, because together those three numbers decide revenue.

AOV formula

Average order value = Revenue / Number of orders

Worked example

Your shop takes £18,000 from 450 orders in a month. Your average order value is £40.

Ways to raise AOV

  • Set a free delivery threshold a little above your current AOV.
  • Offer bundles or multi-buy prices.
  • Suggest related products on the product page and in the basket.
  • Show a better version of the product next to the one being viewed.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter your revenue for a period, such as a month.
  2. Enter the number of orders in the same period.
  3. Read off the average order value.

Leave out refunded and cancelled orders from both figures.

AOV and revenue

Online revenue is the product of three numbers: visitors, conversion rate and average order value. With 10,000 visitors, a 2% conversion rate and an AOV of £40, revenue is £8,000. Raising AOV to £44 adds £800 without a single extra visitor or order.

Bear in mind that an average can hide a lot. A few very large orders can pull it up while most customers spend much less, so look at the spread of order values as well. Compare AOV by channel, device and customer type to see where the valuable orders come from.

Mean and median order value

AOV is a mean, so one very large order can pull it up. The median, the middle order when all are ranked by value, shows what a typical customer spends. If the two differ widely, a few big orders are skewing the average.

Frequently asked questions

Is AOV the same as revenue per customer?

No. One customer may order several times. AOV is measured per order.

Should I include delivery charges and VAT?

Either is fine, as long as you measure it the same way every time.
