What is ROAS?

ROAS means return on ad spend: the revenue earned for every pound spent on advertising. It is the standard way to compare campaigns on Google Ads, Meta, Amazon and other platforms.

ROAS formula

ROAS = Revenue from ads / Ad spend

Worked example

A campaign costs £1,500 and brings in £6,000 of sales. The ROAS is 4x, or 400%: each £1 of advertising produced £4 of revenue.

What ROAS do you need?

ROAS measures revenue, not profit, so the figure you need depends on your margin. Your break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your gross margin. With a 25% margin you need a ROAS of 4x just to cover the cost of the goods and the adverts. With a 50% margin, 2x is enough.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the revenue the adverts produced, as reported by the ad platform or your analytics.
  2. Enter what the adverts cost in the same period.
  3. Read off ROAS as a multiple and a percentage.

Why reported ROAS can mislead

Ad platforms count a sale when someone saw or clicked an advert before buying, even if they would have bought anyway. Two platforms can also claim the same sale. Treat reported ROAS as a guide, and compare total advertising spend with total revenue from time to time as a check.

ROAS and ROI

ROAS only looks at advertising cost against revenue. ROI takes all costs into account and measures profit. A campaign can have a healthy ROAS and still lose money once the cost of the goods, delivery and fees are counted.
