What is customer acquisition cost?

Customer acquisition cost, or CAC, is the average amount you spend to win one new customer. It counts everything spent on bringing customers in, not only advertising.

CAC formula

CAC = (Marketing spend + Sales costs) / New customers

Use spending and new customers from the same period.

Worked example

In a quarter you spend £9,000 on marketing and £6,000 on sales staff and tools, £15,000 in all, and win 300 new customers. Your CAC is £50.

CAC and lifetime value

CAC only means something next to what a customer is worth. Many businesses aim for a customer lifetime value of around three times CAC. If the two figures are close, growth is costing nearly as much as it earns.

CAC and CPA

CPA is usually measured for one campaign and can count any conversion, such as a lead. CAC covers all sales and marketing costs and counts only new paying customers, so it is normally higher.
