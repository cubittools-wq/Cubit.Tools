Percentage change formula

Percentage change = (New price - Old price) / Old price x 100

A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

Worked example

A supplier raises a price from £40 to £46. The difference is £6, which is an increase of 15%.

Passing on a cost increase

If a supplier's price goes up and you raise your own price by the same number of pounds, your profit per sale stays the same but your margin falls slightly. If you raise your price by the same percentage, your margin stays the same and your profit per sale rises. Decide which you are aiming for before you reprice.

If you know the percentage and need the new price, multiply the old price by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. This table shows the result for an item that costs £40.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the price before the change.
  2. Enter the price after the change.
  3. Read off the difference in pounds and the percentage change.

If the new price is lower, both results are shown as negative numbers.

Price rises and inflation

Comparing a price rise with the rate of inflation shows whether it is a real increase. If inflation is 3% and a supplier raises prices by 3%, the price has stayed the same in real terms. A rise of 8% in the same year is a real increase of about 5%. The same check applies to your own prices: a price held flat for several years has fallen in real terms, while your costs have probably risen.

Frequently asked questions

Why isn't a 15% rise cancelled out by a 15% cut?

The two percentages are taken from different starting prices. £40 plus 15% is £46, but £46 less 15% is £39.10.

Can I use this for costs or wages?

Yes. The sum is the same for any two amounts.
