What is contribution margin?

Contribution margin is what is left from a sale after paying the costs that sale caused. That money goes towards fixed costs first, and becomes profit once they are covered.

Contribution margin formula

Contribution per unit = Selling price - Variable cost per unit

Contribution margin ratio = Contribution per unit / Selling price x 100

Worked example

You sell a product for £50 and each one costs £30 in stock, packaging and delivery. Each sale contributes £20, a ratio of 40%. Selling 400 units gives a total contribution of £8,000. If your fixed costs are below that figure, the rest is profit.

How to use it

  • Compare products. The product with the highest contribution per unit is not always the best seller, but it does the most for profit per sale.
  • Judge a discount. A price cut reduces contribution by the full amount of the cut.
  • Find break-even. Divide fixed costs by the contribution per unit.

Contribution margin and gross margin

Gross margin takes off the cost of goods sold, which can include some fixed production costs. Contribution margin takes off only costs that change with each sale, and includes selling costs such as commission and postage.
