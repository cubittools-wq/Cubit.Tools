What is conversion rate?

Conversion rate is the percentage of visitors who complete the action you want, such as placing an order, filling in a form or subscribing to a newsletter.

Conversion rate formula

Conversion rate = Conversions / Visitors x 100

Worked example

Your website has 8,000 visitors in a month and takes 200 orders. Your conversion rate is 2.5%.

Why small changes matter

Raising conversion rate increases sales without paying for more traffic. A shop converting 2.5% of 8,000 visitors takes 200 orders. At 3%, the same visitors give 240 orders: a fifth more sales from the same advertising spend.

Ways to improve conversion rate

  • Make pages load quickly, especially on mobile.
  • Show delivery costs and times before the checkout.
  • Use clear photos, full product details and customer reviews.
  • Cut the number of steps and form fields in the checkout.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of visitors or sessions in a period.
  2. Enter the number of conversions in the same period.
  3. Read off the conversion rate.

Reading your conversion rate

An overall figure hides big differences. Returning visitors convert far better than new ones, desktop often converts better than mobile, and visitors from email or a branded search convert better than those from a display advert. Splitting the rate by source, device and landing page shows where the real problems and opportunities are.

How much data do I need?

Small numbers swing widely. With 100 visitors, one extra order moves the rate by a full percentage point. Judge changes over enough visits for the difference to be more than chance.

Micro-conversions

Not every visitor is ready to buy. Smaller steps, such as adding to the basket, starting the checkout or joining a mailing list, can each be measured as a conversion rate. Tracking them shows the stage at which visitors drop out.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use visitors or sessions?

Either works. Analytics tools often use sessions. Keep to one so your figures can be compared over time.
