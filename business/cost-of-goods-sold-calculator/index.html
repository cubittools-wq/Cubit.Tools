What is cost of goods sold?

Cost of goods sold, or COGS, is the direct cost of the products you sold in a period. Taking it away from your sales gives your gross profit. In UK accounts it is usually called cost of sales.

COGS formula

Cost of goods sold = Opening stock + Purchases - Closing stock

Worked example

You start the year with £12,000 of stock, buy £45,000 more, and have £15,000 left at the end. Your cost of goods sold is £42,000.

What counts towards COGS?

  • Included: the purchase price of stock, delivery to you, import duty, raw materials and direct production labour.
  • Not included: rent, marketing, office salaries and the cost of delivering to customers. These are overheads.

Frequently asked questions

Why does closing stock reduce COGS?

Stock you still hold has not been sold, so its cost belongs to a later period.

Does a service business have COGS?

Often not, or it is called cost of sales and covers the direct cost of delivering the service, such as subcontractors.
