What is CPC?

CPC, cost per click, is the average amount you pay each time someone clicks your advert. It is the basis of pay-per-click advertising on search engines, shopping ads and social media.

CPC formula

CPC = Ad spend / Number of clicks

Worked example

You spend £800 and receive 1,600 clicks. Your average CPC is £0.50.

How much can you afford to pay for a click?

Multiply the profit from a sale by your conversion rate. If a sale leaves £30 of profit and 2% of clicks become sales, a click is worth 60p to you. Paying more than that loses money on the first order.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter what you spent on the campaign, ad group or keyword.
  2. Enter the number of clicks it received.
  3. Read off the average cost of a click.

CPC, CPM and CTR are linked

If you buy advertising by the thousand impressions, you can still work out an effective cost per click. Divide the CPM by 1,000 to get the cost of one impression, then divide by the click-through rate. A £5 CPM with a 1% CTR is an effective CPC of 50p.

Average CPC and maximum CPC

On auction platforms you set the most you are willing to pay for a click. You are usually charged less than that, because the price depends on the next highest bidder, so your average CPC sits below your maximum bid.

Is a low CPC always good?

No. Cheap clicks that never convert cost more in the end than expensive clicks that do.

What affects CPC?

  • Competition: more advertisers bidding on the same search pushes the price up.
  • Relevance: platforms charge less for adverts that people find useful and click on.
  • Targeting: location, device, time of day and audience all change the price.
