What is CPM?

CPM is the cost of one thousand impressions of an advert. The M is the Roman numeral for a thousand. It is how display, video, social and print advertising is usually priced, and it lets you compare the cost of reaching an audience across different channels.

CPM formula

CPM = Ad cost / Impressions x 1,000

Worked example

An advert costs £450 and is shown 150,000 times. The CPM is £3.

CPM, CPC and CPA

CPM measures the cost of being seen, CPC the cost of a click and CPA the cost of a conversion. CPM suits campaigns that aim to build awareness. If you want sales or enquiries, a low CPM is only useful if the people seeing the advert go on to click and buy.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the total cost of the advert or campaign.
  2. Enter the number of impressions it received.
  3. Read off the cost per thousand impressions.

Working backwards from CPM

If you know the CPM, you can work out the other figures:

  • Cost = Impressions / 1,000 x CPM. A campaign of 200,000 impressions at a £4 CPM costs £800.
  • Impressions = Cost / CPM x 1,000. A budget of £600 at a £3 CPM buys 200,000 impressions.

For publishers, the same sum from the other side is called RPM: the revenue earned per thousand page views.

CPM and eCPM

Effective CPM, or eCPM, applies the same sum to advertising bought another way. If you paid per click and spent £200 for adverts that were shown 80,000 times, your eCPM is £2.50. It lets you compare campaigns bought on different pricing models.

Frequently asked questions

What is an impression?

One showing of an advert. The same person can account for several impressions.

Why do CPMs vary so much?

Price depends on the audience, the platform, the ad format and the time of year. Narrowly targeted audiences and busy periods such as the run-up to Christmas cost more.
