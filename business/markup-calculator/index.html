What is markup?

Markup is the amount you add to the cost of a product to reach its selling price, shown as a percentage of the cost. It is the usual way for wholesalers, tradespeople and shops to set prices, because you normally know the cost first.

Markup formula

Selling price = Cost x (1 + Markup / 100)

Markup = (Selling price - Cost) / Cost x 100

Worked example

A product costs you £40 and you add a markup of 50%. The selling price is £60, which gives a profit of £20 on each sale. That profit is 33.33% of the selling price, so the margin is lower than the markup.

Markup and margin are not the same

Both describe the same profit, measured against different numbers. Markup compares profit with cost. Margin compares profit with the selling price. A 50% markup is a 33.33% margin, and a 100% markup is a 50% margin. Mixing the two up is a common reason for prices that look profitable but are not.

Frequently asked questions

What markup do I need for a 50% margin?

100%. You have to double the cost for half of the selling price to be profit.

Does markup include overheads?

Only if you build them into the cost. A markup on the purchase price alone has to be high enough to pay for rent, wages, delivery and payment fees as well.
