Sales commission formula

Commission = Sales value x Commission rate / 100

Total pay = Base pay + Commission

Worked example

A salesperson sells £25,000 in a month on a commission rate of 5%. They earn £1,250 in commission. With base pay of £2,000, their total pay for the month is £3,250 before tax.

Common commission structures

  • Flat rate: the same percentage on every sale, as this calculator uses.
  • Tiered: the rate rises once sales pass a target.
  • Margin based: commission is paid on the profit from a sale, not its value, which discourages heavy discounting.
  • Commission only: no base pay.

Designing a commission plan

A good plan is simple enough for a salesperson to work out their own pay. Decide what you want to reward: revenue, profit, new customers or renewals. Paying on revenue alone can encourage discounting, so many businesses pay on gross profit or reduce the rate on discounted deals. Decide also when commission is earned: when the order is placed, when it is invoiced or when the customer pays.

Commission and the minimum wage

In the UK, a worker's total pay, including commission, must still come to at least the National Minimum Wage for the hours worked in each pay period. An employer has to top up the pay of a commission-only worker whose sales fall short in a slow month.

Frequently asked questions

Is commission taxed?

Yes. In the UK it is treated as earnings and taxed through payroll in the same way as salary.

Should commission be paid on the price with or without VAT?

Normally without VAT, because VAT is not the company's income. Your contract or commission plan should say.
