What is CPA?

CPA, cost per acquisition (or cost per action), is the average amount you pay in advertising for each conversion. A conversion is whatever you count as success: a sale, a sign-up, an enquiry or an app install.

CPA formula

CPA = Campaign cost / Number of conversions

Worked example

You spend £1,200 on a campaign that produces 48 orders. Your CPA is £25.

Is your CPA affordable?

Compare it with the profit from a conversion. If an average order leaves £40 of gross profit, a CPA of £25 still makes money on the first sale, while a CPA of £50 only pays back if customers return and buy again. Working out customer lifetime value shows how much you can afford to pay.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the total cost of the campaign for a period.
  2. Enter the number of conversions it produced in that period.
  3. Read off the cost of each conversion.

Decide what counts as a conversion before you start, and keep to it, so campaigns can be compared.

Target CPA

A target CPA is the most you are willing to pay for a conversion. To set one, start with the profit from a conversion and decide how much of it you are prepared to spend on advertising. If an order leaves £40 of gross profit and you want to keep half, your target CPA is £20. Most ad platforms let you enter a target CPA and will adjust bids to try to meet it.

CPA for leads

If a conversion is an enquiry, not a sale, divide the CPA by the share of leads that become customers. A lead CPA of £10 with one lead in five converting is a customer cost of £50.

How to lower CPA

  • Pause keywords, audiences and placements that spend without converting.
  • Improve the landing page so more visitors convert.
  • Test new adverts regularly against your best performer.
