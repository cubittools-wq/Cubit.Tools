What is staff turnover?

Staff turnover, also called employee or labour turnover, is the percentage of your workforce that leaves in a given period, usually a year.

Staff turnover formula

Average employees = (Employees at start + Employees at end) / 2

Turnover rate = Leavers / Average employees x 100

Worked example

You start the year with 58 employees and end it with 62, an average of 60. During the year 9 people leave. Your staff turnover rate is 15%.

Why it matters

Replacing an employee costs money in recruitment, training and lost output while the role is empty or the new starter is learning. High turnover also puts pressure on the people who stay. Some sectors, such as hospitality and retail, normally see far higher turnover than others.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of employees who left during the period.
  2. Enter the headcount at the start and at the end of the period.
  3. Read off the average headcount and the turnover rate.

Going beyond one number

An overall rate is a starting point. It becomes useful when you break it down:

  • By team or manager, to find where people are leaving.
  • By length of service. Many leavers in the first year points to recruitment or induction problems.
  • By reason, gathered from exit interviews.

Some employers also measure a stability rate: the share of staff with more than a year's service who are still employed a year later.

Monthly and annual rates

If you measure turnover monthly, add the twelve monthly rates together for an annual figure. Do not multiply one month by twelve, because leavers are rarely spread evenly through the year.

Frequently asked questions

Who counts as a leaver?

Usually everyone who left, whatever the reason. Many employers also work out a separate figure for resignations only, as that is the part they can influence.

Is some turnover healthy?

Yes. New people bring new skills, and a rate of zero is neither likely nor necessarily desirable.
