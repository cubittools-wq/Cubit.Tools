What is stock turnover?

Stock turnover, also called inventory turnover, is the number of times in a year that a business sells and replaces its stock. It shows how hard the money tied up in stock is working.

Stock turnover formula

Stock turnover = Cost of goods sold / Average stock value

Days in stock = 365 / Stock turnover

Average stock is usually the opening stock plus the closing stock, divided by two. Both figures should be at cost, not at selling price.

Worked example

Your cost of goods sold for the year is £240,000 and you hold £40,000 of stock on average. Your stock turns over 6x a year, so an item sits on the shelf for about 61 days before it sells.

Is a higher turnover better?

Usually, because less cash is tied up and less stock goes out of date. A very high figure can also mean you are holding too little and running out. Supermarkets turn stock many times a year. Furniture and jewellery shops turn it far more slowly.

How to improve it

  • Order smaller quantities more often.
  • Clear slow lines with promotions.
  • Use sales history to buy closer to real demand.
