What is customer lifetime value?

Customer lifetime value, or CLV, is the total gross profit you can expect from an average customer for as long as they keep buying. It tells you how much you can afford to spend to win and keep a customer.

CLV formula

Lifetime revenue = Average order value x Orders per year x Years as a customer

CLV = Lifetime revenue x Gross margin / 100

Worked example

A customer spends £45 per order, orders 4 times a year and stays for 3 years. Their lifetime revenue is £540. At a gross margin of 40%, their lifetime value is £216.

How to use CLV

  • Set a ceiling for acquisition cost. Spending more than CLV to win a customer loses money.
  • Compare channels. Customers from one source may spend more or stay longer than those from another.
  • Value retention. One extra year as a customer raises CLV as much as a large rise in order value.

Limits of the estimate

This is a simple model that treats every year the same and does not discount future income. It is a guide for planning, not a forecast for any one customer.
