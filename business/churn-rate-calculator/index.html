What is churn rate?

Churn rate is the percentage of customers who stop buying or cancel during a period. It matters most to subscription businesses, where income depends on customers staying.

Churn rate formula

Churn rate = Customers lost / Customers at start x 100

Retention rate = 100 - Churn rate

Customers who joined during the period are left out. Only count losses from the group you started with.

Worked example

You begin the month with 1,200 subscribers and 60 cancel. Your monthly churn rate is 5% and your retention rate is 95%.

Monthly churn adds up

A monthly figure that looks small becomes large over a year. At 5% a month, only about 54% of customers are still with you after twelve months, because each month's loss is taken from a smaller group.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the number of customers at the start of the period.
  2. Enter how many of those customers left during the period.
  3. Read off the churn rate, the retention rate and the number retained.

Customer churn and revenue churn

Customer churn counts people. Revenue churn counts the income they took with them. The two can differ sharply: losing one large customer may matter more than losing ten small ones. Subscription businesses usually track both, and also net revenue churn, which offsets lost income with extra spending from customers who stayed.

What is a good churn rate?

It depends on the market and the price. Low-cost consumer subscriptions lose customers far faster than contracts with large businesses. Your own trend over time is the most useful guide.

Ways to reduce churn

  • Ask leaving customers why, and look for the reasons that repeat.
  • Help new customers get value quickly in their first weeks.
  • Contact customers whose usage or orders are falling before they cancel.
  • Retry failed payments, which cause churn nobody chose.
