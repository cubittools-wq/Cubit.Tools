What is ROI?

Return on investment, or ROI, is the profit from an investment shown as a percentage of what it cost. It lets you compare projects of different sizes on the same basis, whether that is new equipment, a marketing campaign or a stock purchase.

ROI formula

ROI = (Amount returned - Amount invested) / Amount invested x 100

Worked example

You spend £4,000 on a trade show and it brings in £5,200 of gross profit. The profit is £1,200, an ROI of 30%.

Getting a fair figure

  • Count all the costs, including staff time, fees and delivery.
  • Use profit, not sales, for the amount returned when you are measuring marketing. Revenue alone overstates the return.
  • Remember time. ROI takes no account of how long the return took. 30% in six months is better than 30% in three years.

Frequently asked questions

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. A negative ROI means you got back less than you put in.

What is a good ROI?

One that beats what the money could have earned elsewhere at similar risk.
