How to add VAT

VAT (Value Added Tax) is charged as a percentage of the net price. To add it, multiply the net price by the rate and add the result to the net price.

VAT = Net price x Rate / 100

Price including VAT = Net price + VAT

Worked example

A net price of £100 at the standard rate of 20% carries £20 of VAT, so the customer pays £120.

UK VAT rates

  • Standard rate, 20%: most goods and services.
  • Reduced rate, 5%: some goods and services, such as home energy and children's car seats.
  • Zero rate, 0%: most food, books and children's clothes.

Some things are exempt from VAT altogether, which is different from zero rated. Check the rate for your product on GOV.UK if you are unsure.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the price before VAT.
  2. Choose the rate that applies to what you are selling.
  3. Read off the VAT to add and the total to charge.

A quick way to add 20% VAT in your head is to multiply the net price by 1.2. For the 5% reduced rate, multiply by 1.05.

VAT on invoices

A VAT invoice must show the net amount, the rate, the VAT and the total for each line or for the invoice as a whole. If you are VAT registered, the VAT you charge is collected on behalf of HMRC and paid over in your VAT return, less the VAT you have paid on your own purchases.

Frequently asked questions

How do I take VAT off a price?

Don't subtract 20%. Divide the price by 1.2 instead, or use our Reverse VAT Calculator.

Do I have to charge VAT?

Only if your business is registered for VAT. Registration is compulsory once your taxable turnover passes the threshold set by HMRC.
