What is GMROI?

GMROI stands for gross margin return on inventory investment. It tells a retailer how many pounds of gross profit were earned for each pound held in stock, so it combines margin and stock turnover in one figure.

GMROI formula

GMROI = Gross profit / Average stock cost

Worked example

A shop makes £90,000 of gross profit in a year while holding £30,000 of stock on average. Its GMROI is 3.00: every £1 of stock earned £3.00 of gross profit.

How to read it

A GMROI above 1 means the stock earned more gross profit than it cost to hold. Below 1, it earned less. A product with a modest margin that sells quickly can have a better GMROI than a high-margin product that sits on the shelf, which is why the figure is useful for deciding what to stock and how much space to give it.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a good GMROI?

It varies by sector, so compare products, categories or shops within your own business first.

Should stock be at cost or retail value?

At cost. Using retail value would understate the return.
