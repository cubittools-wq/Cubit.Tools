Summer Solstice Countdown

Our Summer Solstice Countdown shows exactly how long is left until the June solstice. The live timer counts down the days, hours, minutes and seconds to the moment of the June solstice.

The June solstice is the moment when the sun reaches its northernmost point in the sky, giving the longest day of the year in the northern hemisphere and the shortest in the southern hemisphere. It marks the astronomical start of summer. In the UK the sun rises before 5am and sets after 9pm in the south, and further north the daylight lasts even longer, with the sky barely getting dark in Shetland.

Thousands of people gather at Stonehenge in Wiltshire each year to watch the sunrise on the solstice, and midsummer is celebrated across Scandinavia and much of Europe with bonfires and dancing.

The longest day

The exact time of the solstice is set by the sun's position and is calculated here in UTC and shown to the nearest minute. The longest period of daylight falls on the date of the solstice, but the hottest weather usually comes a few weeks later, because the Earth takes time to warm up. Dates and seasons given are for the northern hemisphere. In the southern hemisphere the seasons are reversed, so the same date marks the start of the opposite season.
