The Day of the Dead, or DÃ­a de Muertos, is a Mexican holiday on 1 and 2 November on which families remember and celebrate loved ones who have died. They build ofrendas, altars decorated with marigolds, candles, photographs, favourite foods and sugar skulls, and visit cemeteries. The tradition combines indigenous Aztec beliefs with Catholic All Saints' and All Souls' days, and UNESCO added it to the list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008.

Not a Mexican Halloween

The Day of the Dead is a joyful remembrance, not a scary festival, and is separate from Halloween on 31 October, though the dates are close. The countdown is to 1 November, the first day of the celebration, traditionally devoted to the souls of children.
