Understanding Net Profit Margin

Net profit margin displays the percentage of revenue remaining after subtracting all operating expenses, taxes, interest, and costs of goods sold. A higher margin can indicate better expense control and financial sustainability.

Net Profit Margin Formula

Net Profit Margin = ((Total Revenue - Total Costs) / Total Revenue) * 100

For example, if a business generates £100,000 in revenue and has total costs of £80,000, its net profit is £20,000 and its net profit margin is 20%.

How to Calculate Net Profit Margin

To calculate your net profit margin, enter your total revenue and total costs into the calculator. The calculator subtracts your costs from your revenue to determine your net profit, then expresses that profit as a percentage of your total revenue.

What Does Net Profit Margin Tell You?

Net profit margin helps show how much profit a business retains from each pound of revenue after accounting for its costs. For example, a 15% net profit margin means that a business keeps £15 in net profit for every £100 of revenue.

Comparing net profit margins over time can help businesses understand changes in profitability and assess the impact of increasing revenue, reducing costs or changing operating expenses.

Net Margin vs Gross Profit Margin

Gross profit margin measures the profit remaining after the cost of goods sold, while net profit margin takes a broader view and accounts for additional expenses such as operating costs, interest and taxes. Net profit margin therefore provides a more complete picture of overall profitability.
