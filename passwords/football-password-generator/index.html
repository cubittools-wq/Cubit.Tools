How the Football Password Generator works

Choose your club from the menu and the generator builds a passphrase from two sources: words linked to that club, such as nicknames, grounds, famous players, managers and local rivalries, and a list of general football words. Each word is picked at random using your browser's built-in cryptographic random number generator, the same kind of randomness used for security keys, so every result is unpredictable. A number and a symbol are added at the end by default, because many websites ask for both.

If you don't want to pick a club, leave the menu on Any club. That option draws on the words of every club in the list at once, which gives a much larger pool of words to choose from.

The address in your browser updates when you change club, so you can bookmark the generator for your team or send the link to another fan.

How strong is a football password?

A passphrase is strong because its words are chosen at random, not because of what they are about. A password such as Arsenal1886 is weak: anyone who knows which team you support could try it, and club names and founding years are among the first things password-guessing software tries. A passphrase such as Highbury-Offside-Gunners-Volley-42! is far harder to guess, because each word was picked at random from a long list.

Two things make a passphrase stronger:

  • More words. Every extra word multiplies the number of possible passphrases. Use the slider to add words.
  • A bigger word list. Some clubs have more words in their list than others. Any club has the largest list of all.

As a guide, four words are fine for low-risk logins such as forums or fantasy football leagues. For your email, bank or any account that holds personal details, use at least six words, and turn on two-step verification where the site offers it.

Tips for using your new password

  • Use it once. Give every account its own password, so a leak on one site doesn't unlock the others.
  • Don't add personal details. Your birthday, shirt number or the year your club was founded make a password easier to guess, not harder.
  • Consider a password manager. It can store a different passphrase for every site and fill them in for you.
  • Change it if a site is breached. If you hear that a service you use has leaked passwords, generate a new one straight away.

Private by design

Your passphrase is created entirely in your browser. It is never sent to our servers, stored or logged, and nobody else can see it.

Frequently asked questions

Which clubs are included?

Every club in the Premier League, Championship, League One and League Two. The full list is below: pick your club there or from the menu above.

Can I use this for my work or bank password?

Yes, as long as you make it long enough. Set the slider to six words or more, keep the number and symbol turned on, and don't reuse the result anywhere else.

Why does the passphrase change every time?

Each click picks new words at random. If you don't like a result, click Generate New Passphrase until you get one you can remember.
