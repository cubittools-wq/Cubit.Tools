How to work out the cost of running an appliance

Electricity is billed by the kilowatt-hour (kWh), often called a unit. One kWh is the energy used by a 1,000 watt appliance running for one hour. To find the cost, turn the appliance's watts into kilowatts, multiply by the hours it runs, and multiply by the price of a unit.

How to use the calculator

  1. Find the appliance's power in watts on its rating plate.
  2. Estimate how many hours a day it is actually running.
  3. Enter your unit rate in pence per kWh from your bill.

Electricity cost formula

kWh used = Watts / 1,000 x Hours

Cost = kWh used x Price per kWh

Worked example

A 2,000 W fan heater is used for 1.5 hours a day. It uses 3.00 kWh a day. At 28 p per kWh, that costs £0.84 a day, £25.55 a month or £306.60 over a full year of daily use.

These are typical ranges. The rating plate on your own appliance gives its actual figure.

Why real costs can be lower

The wattage on the plate is the most an appliance draws. Fridges, ovens, irons and heaters with thermostats switch on and off to hold a temperature, and washing machines only heat for part of the cycle. For these, the energy label or a plug-in energy monitor gives a truer picture.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find my electricity price?

Your unit rate, in pence per kWh, is on your bill and in your supplier's app. The daily standing charge is separate and is not affected by how much you use.
