How many rolls of wallpaper do I need?

A standard roll of wallpaper sold in the UK is 10.05 metres long and 0.53 metres wide, so it covers a little over 5 m². This calculator divides the area of your walls by the area of a roll and adds an allowance for trimming and for lining up the pattern.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the width of each wall you are papering and add them up.
  2. Measure the height from skirting board to ceiling.
  3. Check the roll size on the label and change it if it is not the standard size.

Wallpaper formula

Wall area = Distance around the room x Wall height

Rolls = Wall area x (1 + Allowance / 100) / (Roll length x Roll width), rounded up.

Worked example

The walls to be papered measure 14 m around and are 2.4 m high, an area of 33.6 m². One roll covers 5.33 m². Adding 15% for waste and pattern matching, you need 8 rolls.

Pattern repeat

A plain or random-match paper wastes very little, and 10% is usually enough. A paper with a large pattern has to be lined up from one strip to the next, so part of every strip is cut off and thrown away. The bigger the repeat printed on the label, the more you lose: allow 15% to 25%, or an extra roll for a repeat of more than 50 cm.

Tips before you buy

  • Buy all the rolls at once and check that the batch number on each label is the same. Colours vary between batches.
  • Measure the height at several points. Ceilings are rarely level.
  • For a feature wall, enter the width of that one wall as the distance around the room.
  • Many retailers will refund unopened rolls, so one spare is cheap insurance.

Frequently asked questions

Should I deduct doors and windows?

Usually not. The strips above a door and around a window still use most of a length, and the leftover gives you a margin for mistakes.
