What is a kilowatt-hour?

A kilowatt-hour, written kWh, is a unit of energy. It is the amount used by something with a power of one kilowatt (1,000 watts) running for one hour. Electricity and gas bills are charged per kWh, which is why suppliers also call it a unit.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the power of the appliance in watts.
  2. Enter how many hours it runs in a day.
  3. Read off the energy used per day, per month and per year.

If the power is given in kilowatts, multiply by 1,000 first: 2.2 kW is 2,200 watts.

kWh formula

kWh = Watts x Hours / 1,000

Dividing by 1,000 converts watts into kilowatts.

Worked example

A television with a power of 150 W is on for 5 hours a day. It uses 0.75 kWh a day, which is 22.8 kWh a month and 274 kWh a year.

Watts and kilowatt-hours are different things

Watts measure power: how fast energy is being used at any moment. Kilowatt-hours measure energy: the total used over a period. A 3,000 watt kettle has high power but runs for only a few minutes, so it uses little energy each time. A 100 watt appliance left on all day uses far more.

One kWh in everyday terms

One kilowatt-hour is roughly enough to:

  • run a 10 watt LED bulb for 100 hours;
  • run a 2,000 watt fan heater for 30 minutes;
  • boil a full 3,000 watt kettle about eight times, at two and a half minutes each;
  • run a 100 watt television for 10 hours.

Frequently asked questions

How do I turn kWh into cost?

Multiply by your unit price. At 28p per kWh, 10 kWh costs £2.80. Our Electricity Cost Calculator does this for you.

What is the difference between kW and kWh?

kW is power and kWh is energy. Power multiplied by time gives energy.
