What is standby power?

Many devices keep drawing a little electricity when they are switched off at the remote but still plugged in. This is standby power, sometimes called vampire or phantom power. It runs things like remote control receivers, clocks, network connections and instant-on features.

How to use the calculator

  1. Add up the standby power of the devices you leave plugged in.
  2. Enter how many hours a day they sit on standby.
  3. Enter your electricity price in pence per kWh.

Standby cost formula

kWh per year = Standby watts x Hours per day x 365 / 1,000

Cost per year = kWh per year x Price per kWh

Worked example

A television, set-top box and games console together draw 8 W on standby and are left that way for 20 hours a day. Over a year that is 58.4 kWh, which costs £16.35 at 28 p per kWh.

Which devices use the most?

  • Low: modern televisions and phone chargers with nothing attached usually draw well under 1 watt.
  • Medium: smart speakers, printers, microwaves with clocks and older audio equipment draw a few watts.
  • Higher: set-top boxes, games consoles with instant-on enabled and older appliances can draw 10 watts or more.

Products sold in the UK and EU in recent years have to meet limits on standby power, so newer equipment is generally much better than old.

How to cut standby use

Switch devices off at the wall, or use an extension lead with individual switches so one press cuts power to several. Smart plugs can do it on a timer. Check the power settings on consoles and set-top boxes for an energy-saving standby mode. Leave on anything that needs to stay connected, such as a router or a recorder set to record.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure standby power?

A plug-in energy monitor, which sits between the plug and the socket, shows the watts a device is drawing.
