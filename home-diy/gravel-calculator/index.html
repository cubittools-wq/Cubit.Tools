How to work out how much gravel you need

Gravel is sold by weight, but the amount you need depends on volume. Multiply the length of the area by its width and by the depth of gravel to get cubic metres, then multiply by the weight of a cubic metre of the gravel you are buying.

Gravel formula

Volume (m³) = Length (m) x Width (m) x Depth (mm) / 1,000

Tonnes = Volume x Weight per cubic metre

Worked example

A drive is 6 m long and 3 m wide, and you want gravel 50 mm deep. The volume is 0.90 m³. At 1.6 tonnes per m³, that is 1.44 tonnes of gravel, or 2 bags of 850 kg each.

How deep should gravel be?

  • Paths and borders: 30 mm to 40 mm is enough for foot traffic.
  • Driveways: around 50 mm, on a firm compacted sub-base.
  • The depth should be at least twice the size of the stones. A 20 mm gravel needs 40 mm or more to cover properly.

Too deep is as much a problem as too shallow: feet and tyres sink in.

Bulk bags and loose loads

A bulk bag, sometimes called a tonne bag or dumpy bag, usually holds between 800 kg and 900 kg. Check the weight with your supplier and change the figure above if it differs. For larger jobs, a loose tipped load is often cheaper per tonne, provided a lorry can reach the site.

Frequently asked questions

Does the type of stone matter?

Yes. Dense stone such as granite weighs more per cubic metre than slate chippings or lightweight aggregates. Wet gravel also weighs more than dry.

Do I need a membrane?

A weed-control membrane under the gravel is usual. It stops weeds and keeps the stone from mixing with the soil.
