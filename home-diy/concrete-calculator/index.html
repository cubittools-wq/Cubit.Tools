How to calculate concrete volume

Concrete is ordered by volume, in cubic metres. For a rectangular slab, multiply the length by the width by the depth, with all three in metres. Depth is normally measured in millimetres, so the calculator converts it for you.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the slab in metres.
  2. Enter the depth of concrete in millimetres.
  3. Leave the allowance at 10% unless your supplier advises otherwise.

For a circular base, work out the area as 3.14 times the radius squared, enter that as the length and set the width to 1.

Concrete formula

Volume (m³) = Length (m) x Width (m) x Depth (mm) / 1,000

Concrete to order = Volume x (1 + Allowance / 100)

Worked example

A shed base is 4 m long, 3 m wide and 100 mm deep. The volume is 1.20 m³. Adding 10% for uneven ground and spillage, you should order 1.32 m³, which weighs about 3.17 tonnes.

Typical slab depths

  • 75 mm to 100 mm: paths, patios and bases for small sheds.
  • 100 mm: a base for a larger shed, summerhouse or greenhouse.
  • 100 mm to 150 mm: driveways and garage floors, often with steel mesh.

These are common guide figures. The right depth depends on the ground and the load, and structural work should follow an engineer's or building control's advice.

Ready-mix, mix on site or bags?

For anything over about one cubic metre, ready-mixed concrete delivered by lorry is usually easiest. Smaller jobs can be mixed on site from cement and ballast, or from pre-mixed bags for very small quantities such as fence posts. Remember the sub-base: a slab normally sits on a layer of compacted hardcore, which you need to order separately.

Frequently asked questions

Why add 10%?

The ground is never perfectly level, formwork bows slightly and some concrete is always lost. Running short part-way through a pour is far more costly than having a little left over.
