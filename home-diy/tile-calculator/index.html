How to calculate the number of tiles

Work out the area you are tiling in square metres, divide it by the area of one tile, and add a percentage for the tiles that will be cut at the edges or broken. This calculator does all three steps and rounds the answer up to a whole tile.

How to use the calculator

  1. Measure the length and width of the floor, or the width and height of the wall, in metres.
  2. Enter the size of one tile in centimetres.
  3. Choose an allowance for cuts and breakages.

Tile formula

Area = Length x Width

Tiles = Area / (Tile length x Tile width) x (1 + Allowance / 100)

Tile sizes are entered in centimetres and converted to metres for you.

Worked example

A bathroom floor measures 3.2 m by 2.5 m, an area of 8.00 m². Using tiles of 30 cm by 30 cm, there are 11.1 tiles to the square metre. With 10% added for cuts and breakages, you need 98 tiles.

How much extra to allow

  • 10% for a simple square or rectangular area laid in straight rows.
  • 15% for a diagonal or herringbone layout, a room with lots of corners, or large-format tiles.
  • Keep a few spare tiles after the job. A matching tile may be impossible to find later if one cracks.

Tiles sold by the box

Tiles are usually sold in boxes that cover a stated area. Divide the number of tiles you need by the number in a box and round up. Check that every box has the same batch and shade number.

Frequently asked questions

Should I allow for grout lines?

Grout joints of 2 to 3 mm make very little difference to the count, so the calculator ignores them. That adds a small safety margin.

What about an L-shaped room?

Split it into two rectangles, work out each one and add the results.
