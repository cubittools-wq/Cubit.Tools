What is Ohm's law?

Ohm's law describes how voltage, current and resistance are related in an electrical circuit. The current through a conductor is the voltage across it divided by its resistance. It is named after the German physicist Georg Ohm, who published it in 1827.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the voltage across the component or circuit.
  2. Enter its resistance in ohms.
  3. Read off the current and the power it will dissipate.

For a resistor, check that the power is within its rating. A common small resistor is rated at a quarter of a watt.

Ohm's law formulas

Current (A) = Voltage (V) / Resistance (Ω)

The same relationship can be rearranged to find either of the other values:

Voltage = Current x Resistance

Resistance = Voltage / Current

Power follows from it: Power (W) = Voltage x Current.

Worked example

A 12 V supply is connected across a resistance of 6 Ω. The current is 2 A, and the power used is 24.00 W.

An everyday way to picture it

Think of water in a pipe. Voltage is the pressure pushing the water, current is how much water flows, and resistance is how narrow the pipe is. More pressure pushes more water through. A narrower pipe lets less through at the same pressure.

Where Ohm's law is used

  • Choosing a resistor to limit the current through an LED.
  • Working out how much current a heating element will draw.
  • Finding the voltage lost along a length of cable.
  • Checking that a component will not be overloaded.

Frequently asked questions

Does Ohm's law work for everything?

No. It holds for resistors and most metal conductors at a steady temperature. Components such as LEDs, diodes and filament lamps do not have a fixed resistance, so the simple formula only gives an approximation.

What is an ohm?

The unit of resistance. One ohm lets one amp flow when one volt is applied.
