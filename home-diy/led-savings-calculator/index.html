How much do LED bulbs save?

An LED bulb gives the same light as a halogen or filament bulb for a small fraction of the electricity. The saving depends on the difference in wattage, how many bulbs you change and how long they are on each day.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the wattage of the bulbs you have now and of the LEDs that will replace them.
  2. Enter how many bulbs you are changing and how long they are on each day.
  3. Enter your electricity price and the cost of one LED bulb.

LED savings formula

kWh saved per year = (Old watts - LED watts) x Number of bulbs x Hours per day x 365 / 1,000

Saving per year = kWh saved x Price per kWh

Worked example

A kitchen has 10 halogen spotlights of 50 W each, on for 4 hours a day. Replacing them with 5 W LEDs saves 657 kWh a year. At 28 p per kWh, that is £183.96 a year. If each LED costs £3, the new bulbs pay for themselves in 2.0 months.

Other benefits of LEDs

  • Longer life. A good LED is rated for 15,000 hours or more, compared with around 2,000 hours for a halogen, so you replace bulbs far less often.
  • Less heat. Halogen bulbs turn most of their energy into heat. LEDs run much cooler.
  • Choice of light colour, from warm white to daylight.

Things to check before you switch

Match the cap type (such as GU10, B22 or E27) and compare lumens to get the same brightness. If the lights are on a dimmer, buy dimmable LEDs and check that the dimmer is suitable for LED loads, as older dimmers can cause flicker. Low-voltage MR16 spotlights may also need a compatible transformer.

Frequently asked questions

Are halogen bulbs still sold?

Most mains-voltage halogen bulbs have been phased out of sale in the UK, so LED is now the usual replacement.
