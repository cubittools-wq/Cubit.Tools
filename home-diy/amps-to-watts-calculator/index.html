How to convert amps to watts

Power in watts is the current in amps multiplied by the voltage. Amps measure how much electricity is flowing, volts measure the pressure pushing it, and watts measure the rate at which energy is used.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the current in amps, from the rating plate, a fuse or a clamp meter reading.
  2. Check the voltage. Leave it at 230 for UK mains, or enter 12 for a car or caravan system.
  3. Read off the power in watts and kilowatts.

How many watts can a 13 amp socket supply?

About 3,000 watts. A double socket is rated for 13 amps across both outlets on most designs, not 26.

Amps to watts formula

Watts = Amps x Volts

This applies to direct current and to simple resistive loads on mains electricity, such as heaters, kettles and filament lamps.

Worked example

A standard UK plug is fused at up to 13 A. At a mains voltage of 230 V, the most a single socket can supply is 2,990 W, or 2.99 kW. That is why a 3 kW kettle or fan heater is the largest appliance you will find on a 13 amp plug.

Motors and other appliances

Appliances with motors or electronic power supplies draw slightly more current than this sum suggests, because of an effect called power factor. For those, real power is amps times volts times the power factor, a number between 0 and 1. The rating plate on the appliance gives the true figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is UK mains 230 V or 240 V?

The official figure is 230 volts, within an allowed range. In practice many UK homes measure closer to 240 volts.
