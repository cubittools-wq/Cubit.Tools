How to work out how much paint you need

The amount of paint depends on three things: the area of the walls, the number of coats and how far a litre of the paint goes. Measure the width of each wall you are painting and add them together, then multiply by the height of the room. Take off the area of doors and windows, because you will not be painting those.

Paint formula

Area to paint = (Total wall length x Wall height) - Doors and windows

Litres needed = Area to paint x Number of coats / Coverage

Worked example

A room has 14 m of wall in total and the walls are 2.4 m high, which is 33.6 m². Taking off 3.5 m² for a door and a window leaves 30.1 m² to paint. With 2 coats of a paint that covers 12 m² per litre, you need 5.0 litres. That is a 5 litre tin with nothing to spare, so a small extra tin is sensible.

Typical sizes and coverage

  • A standard internal door is about 1.6 m² and an average window is about 1.5 to 2 m².
  • Most emulsions cover 10 to 13 m² per litre. The figure for your paint is printed on the tin.
  • Bare plaster, textured walls and strong colour changes all use more paint.

How many coats?

Two coats is normal for walls. New plaster needs a thinned mist coat first, which soaks in and seals the surface. Painting a pale colour over a dark one may take three coats, or a coat of primer and two of colour.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work for ceilings?

Yes. Enter the length of the ceiling as the wall length and its width as the height, and set doors and windows to 0.

How much extra should I buy?

About 10% more than the result covers spills, the paint left in the roller and tray, and touch-ups later. Keeping some from the same batch avoids slight colour differences.
