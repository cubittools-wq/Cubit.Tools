How much light does a room need?

The amount of light falling on a surface is measured in lux. One lux is one lumen spread over one square metre. To find the total light a room needs, multiply its floor area by the lux level suited to what the room is used for.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the length and width of the room in metres.
  2. Choose the type of room.
  3. Enter the lumens of the bulbs you plan to use, from the packaging.

Room lighting formula

Lumens needed = Floor area (m²) x Lux level

Number of bulbs = Lumens needed / Lumens per bulb, rounded up.

Worked example

A living room measures 4.5 m by 3.6 m, a floor area of 16.20 m². At 150 lux it needs 2,430 lm of light. Using bulbs of 806 lm each, that is 4 bulbs.

Typical light levels for a home

  • 100 lux: hallways, landings and stairs.
  • 150 lux: living rooms and bedrooms, for relaxed general lighting.
  • 200 lux: bathrooms.
  • 300 lux: kitchens and utility rooms.
  • 500 lux: desks, workshops and anywhere detailed work is done.

Getting the lighting right

The total is a starting point. Dark walls and floors absorb light, so a room decorated in deep colours needs more. High ceilings also need more, because the light has further to travel. Several lower-powered lights spread around a room give a more even result than one bright fitting in the middle, and a dimmer lets you turn a bright room down in the evening.

Frequently asked questions

Do lamps and spotlights count?

Yes. Add up the lumens of every light source in the room.

What about task lighting?

Worktops, desks and mirrors benefit from their own light, such as under-cabinet strips or a desk lamp, on top of the general lighting.
