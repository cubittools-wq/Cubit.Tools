How to convert watts to amps

The current an appliance draws, in amps, is its power in watts divided by the voltage of the supply. Watts measure how fast energy is used, volts measure the electrical pressure and amps measure the flow of electricity.

Watts to amps formula

Amps = Watts / Volts

Worked example

A 2,000 watt fan heater is plugged into a UK socket at 230 volts. Dividing 2,000 by 230 gives 8.7 amps, comfortably within the 13 amp limit of a standard plug.

Why the current matters

  • Fuses. A UK plug takes a fuse of up to 13 amps. Appliances under about 700 watts normally use a 3 amp fuse, and larger ones a 13 amp fuse.
  • Extension leads. Add up the current of everything plugged into a lead. The total must not exceed its rating, usually 13 amps.
  • Circuits. Large loads such as cookers, showers and car chargers need their own circuit with a suitably rated cable and breaker.

Motors and electronics

The formula is exact for heaters, kettles and other simple resistive loads. Appliances with motors or electronic power supplies draw a little more current than their wattage suggests, because of power factor. The current on the rating plate is the figure to rely on.

Frequently asked questions

What voltage should I enter?

230 volts for UK and European mains, 120 volts for North America, or 12 volts for most car and caravan systems.

Can I use this to choose a cable size?

No. Cable sizing also depends on the length of the run, how the cable is installed and the protective device. That is a job for a qualified electrician.
