Lumens and watts measure different things

Lumens measure how much light a bulb gives out. Watts measure how much electricity it uses. With old filament bulbs the two went hand in hand, so people learned to judge brightness by wattage. Modern LED bulbs give the same light for a fraction of the power, so lumens are now the figure to shop by.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter the brightness you want in lumens. The figure is printed on bulb packaging.
  2. Choose the type of bulb.
  3. Read off the approximate power it uses.

Switch between bulb types to compare the wattage needed for the same light.

Lumens to watts formula

Watts = Lumens / Lumens per watt

Lumens per watt is the efficiency of the bulb, called luminous efficacy. It varies by technology and from one product to another.

Worked example

You want a bulb as bright as an old 60 watt filament bulb, which gives about 806 lm. An LED producing 100 lumens per watt needs only 8.1 W to give the same light.

Choosing a bulb

  • Pick the lumens first, to get the brightness you want.
  • Then check the colour temperature in kelvin: around 2700 K is warm white, 4000 K is cool white and 6500 K is daylight.
  • Check the cap type and whether the bulb is dimmable.

Frequently asked questions

Why do two LED bulbs with the same lumens use different watts?

Efficiency differs between products. Newer and better-quality LEDs produce more light per watt.
