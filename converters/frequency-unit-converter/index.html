What is frequency?

Frequency is how often something repeats in a given time. It is measured in hertz, the number of complete cycles per second. Sound, radio waves, alternating current and the vibration of a guitar string are all described by their frequency.

Everyday frequencies

  • 50 Hz: mains electricity in the UK and Europe. North America uses 60 Hz.
  • 20 Hz to 20 kHz: the range of human hearing.
  • 440 Hz: the note A above middle C, used to tune instruments.
  • 88 to 108 MHz: FM radio.
  • 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz: the two main Wi-Fi bands.

Frequency and rpm

Rotational speed is a frequency too. Divide revolutions per minute by 60 to get hertz. An engine turning at 3,000 rpm completes 50 revolutions each second, which is 50 Hz.

About the units

  • Hertz: One cycle per second, written Hz. UK mains electricity alternates at 50 Hz.
  • Kilohertz: 1,000 Hz. Used for audio and for AM radio.
  • Megahertz: One million Hz. Used for FM radio and older computer processors.
  • Gigahertz: One thousand million Hz. Used for Wi-Fi, mobile networks and processor speeds.
  • Revolutions per minute: How many full turns something makes in a minute. 60 rpm is 1 Hz.

Period

The period is the time one cycle takes, and it is 1 divided by the frequency. A 50 Hz supply has a period of 0.02 seconds, or 20 milliseconds. A 1 kHz tone has a period of 1 millisecond.

Frequently asked questions

How is frequency related to wavelength?

They are inversely related. The higher the frequency, the shorter the wavelength. For radio waves, wavelength in metres is roughly 300 divided by the frequency in megahertz.

Is a higher GHz processor always faster?

Not necessarily. Clock speed is one factor, alongside the number of cores and the design of the chip.
