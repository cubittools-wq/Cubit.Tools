Cooking and Baking Measurement Conversion

Accurate kitchen measurements ensure recipe success whether baking or cooking. Easily convert between volumetric units like cups and tablespoons, liquid volume like milliliters, and dry weight measurements like grams and ounces.

Common Kitchen Conversions

  • 1 Cup = 16 Tablespoons = 240 Milliliters
  • 1 Tablespoon = 3 Teaspoons = 15 Milliliters
  • 1 Fluid Ounce = 29.57 Milliliters

Cups measure volume, not weight

This converter changes one volume into another. It cannot tell you what a cup of an ingredient weighs, because that depends on the ingredient: a cup of water weighs about 240 g, a cup of plain flour about 120 g and a cup of granulated sugar about 200 g. For baking, weighing ingredients in grams is more reliable than measuring by the cup.

Following an American recipe

American recipes use the US cup of 240 ml, along with US fluid ounces and US pints, which are smaller than the British ones. A US pint is 473 ml and a UK pint is 568 ml. Spoon sizes are effectively the same on both sides of the Atlantic.

About the units

  • Teaspoons: A 5 ml measuring spoon, written tsp.
  • Tablespoons: A 15 ml measuring spoon, written tbsp. Three teaspoons make one tablespoon.
  • US cups: 240 ml. The cup used in American recipes.
  • Metric cups: 250 ml. Used in Australia, New Zealand and some UK measuring sets.
  • US fluid ounces: About 29.6 ml.
  • UK fluid ounces: About 28.4 ml. There are 20 in a UK pint.
  • UK pints: 568 ml.
  • Millilitres: The metric unit used in most UK recipes.
  • Litres: 1,000 millilitres.

Frequently asked questions

How many tablespoons are in a cup?

16 tablespoons in a US cup.

Should a spoonful be level or heaped?

Level, unless the recipe says otherwise.
