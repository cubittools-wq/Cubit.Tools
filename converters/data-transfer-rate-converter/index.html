Megabits and megabytes are not the same

Broadband is sold in megabits per second (Mbps), but files are measured in megabytes (MB). There are 8 bits in a byte, so divide a speed in Mbps by 8 to see how many megabytes arrive each second. This is the reason a download on an 80 Mbps connection shows about 10 MB/s, not 80.

Worked example

You have a 100 Mbps connection and want to download a 4 GB file. 100 Mbps is 12.5 MB/s. The file is 4,000 MB, so it takes 4,000 divided by 12.5, which is 320 seconds, or a little over 5 minutes at full speed.

What speed do you need?

  • Video calls and HD streaming: around 5 Mbps per stream.
  • 4K streaming: around 25 Mbps per stream.
  • A busy household: add up what is likely to be in use at once.

These are typical figures quoted by streaming services. Upload speed, which is often much lower than download, matters for video calls and backing up files.

About the units

  • Megabits per second: Written Mbps or Mbit/s. The unit broadband speeds are advertised in.
  • Megabytes per second: Written MB/s. The unit download managers and file copies usually show. One MB/s is 8 Mbps.
  • Gigabits per second: 1,000 Mbps. Used for full-fibre broadband and network equipment.
  • Kilobits per second: 1,000 bits per second. Used for audio streams and slow connections.
  • Kilobytes per second: 8,000 bits per second.
  • Bits per second: The base unit of data speed.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my real speed lower than advertised?

Wi-Fi signal, the number of devices sharing the connection, the time of day and the speed of the server at the other end all reduce it.

Is Mb the same as MB?

No. A lower-case b means bits and a capital B means bytes.
