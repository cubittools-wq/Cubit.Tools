What is flow rate?

Flow rate is the volume of liquid or gas passing a point in a given time. It tells you how quickly a tap fills a bath, how much water a pump can move or how fast a pond is circulated.

How to measure the flow from a tap

Hold a measuring jug or a bucket of known size under the tap, turn it on fully and time how long it takes to fill. Divide the volume in litres by the time in seconds and multiply by 60. A 10 litre bucket that fills in 40 seconds means a flow of 15 litres per minute.

Typical household flow rates

  • Kitchen or basin tap: 4 to 10 litres per minute.
  • Electric shower: 3 to 8 litres per minute, depending on its power and the season.
  • Mixer or power shower: 8 to 15 litres per minute or more.
  • Garden hose: around 15 litres per minute.

These are typical ranges and vary with water pressure.

About the units

  • Litres per minute: The usual unit for taps, showers and domestic pumps in the UK.
  • Litres per second: 60 litres per minute. Used for larger pumps and drainage.
  • Cubic metres per hour: 1,000 litres an hour. Used for pool pumps, ventilation and industrial plant.
  • UK gallons per minute: 4.546 litres per minute.
  • US gallons per minute: 3.785 litres per minute, written gpm on American products.

Frequently asked questions

Is flow rate the same as water pressure?

No. Pressure is the force pushing the water and flow rate is the amount that comes out. Good pressure through a narrow pipe can still give a poor flow.

Which gallon do American products use?

The US gallon. A shower head rated at 2.5 gpm in the United States delivers about 9.5 litres per minute.
