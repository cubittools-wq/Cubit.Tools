What is force?

A force is a push or a pull. It is what makes an object speed up, slow down or change direction. Newton's second law states that force equals mass multiplied by acceleration, so a newton is defined as the force that accelerates a one kilogram mass at one metre per second squared.

Force and weight

Weight is the force of gravity on an object. On Earth, a mass of 1 kg weighs about 9.81 newtons, which is one kilogram-force. That is why loads are sometimes loosely given in kilograms: a rope rated at 10 kN will hold a static load of roughly 1,020 kg.

Worked example

A climbing carabiner is marked 24 kN. In pounds-force that is 24 x 224.81, or about 5,395 lbf, and in kilograms-force it is about 2,447 kgf.

About the units

  • Newtons: The metric unit of force, written N. The force needed to accelerate one kilogram by one metre per second, every second.
  • Kilonewtons: 1,000 newtons, written kN. Used for structural loads, anchors and climbing equipment.
  • Pounds-force: The imperial unit, written lbf: the weight of one pound under standard gravity.
  • Kilograms-force: The weight of one kilogram under standard gravity, written kgf. About 9.81 newtons.

Frequently asked questions

Is a pound a unit of force or mass?

Both names exist. The pound (lb) is a unit of mass and the pound-force (lbf) is the weight of that mass. In everyday use people say pounds for either.

Why do ratings use kilonewtons and not kilograms?

Because a moving or falling load exerts far more force than its weight. A rating in kN describes the force the equipment can withstand, however it is produced.
