Temperature Conversion

Temperature conversions are widely used in cooking, weather forecasting, science, and engineering. Easily convert between Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin.

Common Temperature Formulas

  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: (°F - 32) × 5/9 = °C
  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: (°C × 9/5) + 32 = °F
  • Celsius to Kelvin: °C + 273.15 = K

A quick way to estimate

To turn Celsius into Fahrenheit in your head, double it and add 30. The result is close enough for weather: 20 °C becomes 70 °F by this method, against a true figure of 68 °F. To go the other way, subtract 30 and halve.

Worked example

A recipe from the United States says to bake at 350 °F. Subtract 32 to get 318, multiply by 5 to get 1,590, and divide by 9 to get 176.7 °C. On a British oven you would set 180 °C, or 160 °C for a fan oven.

About the units

  • Celsius (°C): Water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C. Used for weather and everyday temperatures in the UK and most of the world.
  • Fahrenheit (°F): Water freezes at 32 °F and boils at 212 °F. Used in the United States and still seen on older British ovens and thermometers.
  • Kelvin (K): The scientific scale, which starts at absolute zero, the coldest temperature possible. A change of one kelvin is the same size as a change of one degree Celsius.

Frequently asked questions

At what temperature are Celsius and Fahrenheit the same?

At minus 40. It is the one point where the two scales cross.

Why is there no degree sign for kelvin?

The kelvin is a unit in its own right, so temperatures are written as 300 K, not 300 °K.

Is centigrade the same as Celsius?

Yes. Centigrade is the older name for the Celsius scale.
