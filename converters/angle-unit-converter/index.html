Degrees or radians on a calculator

Scientific calculators and spreadsheets work in one mode or the other. Spreadsheet functions such as SIN and COS expect radians, so an angle in degrees must be converted first. A wrong mode is one of the most common causes of a trigonometry answer that looks nonsensical.

About the units

  • Degrees: A full circle is 360 degrees. Used in everyday geometry, navigation and building.
  • Radians: A full circle is 2π radians, about 6.283. Used in mathematics, physics and programming.
  • Gradians: A full circle is 400 gradians, so a right angle is 100. Used in some surveying.
