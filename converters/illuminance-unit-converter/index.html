What is illuminance?

Illuminance is the amount of light landing on a surface. It is different from the light a lamp gives out, which is measured in lumens. The same lamp produces a high illuminance on a desk just below it and a much lower one on the far wall, because the light spreads out as it travels.

Lux and foot-candles

Both units describe lumens per unit of area. Lux uses square metres and foot-candles use square feet. As a square metre is about 10.76 square feet, one foot-candle equals 10.76 lux. For a quick estimate, multiply foot-candles by 10.

About the units

  • Lux: The metric unit of illuminance: one lumen falling on one square metre. Used in the UK and Europe.
  • Foot-candles: One lumen falling on one square foot, written fc. Used in the United States.

Where the conversion comes up

Lighting standards and product data from the United States are usually written in foot-candles, while British and European guidance uses lux. Photographers, film crews, growers using horticultural lighting and anyone comparing an American lighting specification with a European one need to move between the two. A light meter can normally be switched to show either unit.

Why does light fall off with distance?

Light spreads over a larger area as it travels. Doubling the distance from a small source spreads the same light over four times the area, so the illuminance drops to a quarter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure lux?

With a light meter, also called a lux meter, placed on the surface you want to measure. Phone apps give a rough reading.

How do lumens relate to lux?

Divide the lumens reaching a surface by its area in square metres. 1,000 lumens spread evenly over 10 m² gives 100 lux.
